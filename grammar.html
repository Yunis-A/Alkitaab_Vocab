Roots & patterns

Most Arabic content words are built from a three-consonant root (جذر) slotted into a pattern (وزن). The root carries the core meaning; the pattern adds grammatical information.

  • Root ك ت ب → writing family: كتب، كتاب، مكتبة، مكتوب
  • Root د ر س → studying family: درس، مدرسة، درّس، دراسة
  • Look up words by root in Lookup to see related vocabulary together.

Iḍāfa (إضافة)

An iḍāfa is a noun–noun possessive/construct chain. The first noun drops nunation / the definite article; definiteness comes from the last noun.

  • كتاب الطالب — the student’s book
  • جامعة القاهرة — Cairo University
  • Adjectives agreeing with the first noun usually follow the whole chain.

Verb forms (الأوزان)

Derived forms alter a Form I root’s meaning (causative, reflexive, intensive, etc.). Knowing the form helps you guess meaning and conjugation patterns.

  • Form I — basic: كتب، درس، ذهب
  • Form II — causative/intensive (doubled middle radical): درّس “to teach”
  • Form IV — causative with أ: أحبّ “to love”
  • Form V — reflexive of II: تكلّم “to speak”
  • Past stem ≈ perfect; present stem ≈ imperfect. Imperative is built from the present.

Gender & number

Nouns and adjectives are masculine or feminine, and singular / dual / plural. Many feminine singulars end in ة (taa’ marbuuṭa).

  • Sound masculine plural: ون / ين (e.g. معلّمون)
  • Sound feminine plural: ات (e.g. جامعات)
  • Broken plurals change the internal pattern: كتاب → كتب
  • Adjectives agree in gender, number, and definiteness with the noun they modify.

Prepositions & verb governance

Some verbs take a fixed preposition that changes meaning. Always learn the preposition with the verb.

  • ذهب إلى — go to (destination)
  • سكن في — live / reside in
  • تكلّم مع / عن — speak with / about
  • Prepositions are followed by a noun in the genitive (مجرور).
